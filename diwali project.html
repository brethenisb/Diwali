How Diwali Is Celebrated

From the first light of dawn to the last diya at night, these are the traditions families follow.

🪔

Lighting Deepams

Clay lamps filled with oil or ghee and a cotton wick are lit at doorsteps, windows and courtyards at dusk. The light drives away darkness and invites Goddess Lakshmi in.

👗

Wearing New Clothes

Families buy new sarees, kurtas, dhotis and children's outfits weeks in advance. The clothes are placed before the deity, then worn on Diwali morning as a sign of a fresh start.

🛁

Early Oil Bath

Before sunrise, elders apply sesame oil and everyone bathes, known as Ganga Snanam in the South. In Tamil homes, people greet each other that morning by asking whether the Ganga bath is done.

🌸

Rangoli and Kolam

Colourful powders, flower petals or rice-flour patterns are drawn at the entrance. They welcome guests and bring good fortune into the house.

🪷

Lakshmi Puja

In the evening, the cleaned and decorated home hosts a puja to Lakshmi and Ganesha with flowers, sweets, coins and fruits. Many shopkeepers also worship their new account books.

🍬

Sweets and Savouries

Ladoo, jalebi, adhirasam, murukku and mysore pak are made at home and shared. In South India, families also take Deepavali lehiyam, a herbal mix, to help digest the feast.

🎆

Crackers and Fireworks

Children and elders light sparklers and crackers at dawn and after dusk. Choose low-noise green crackers, keep a safe distance and keep a bucket of water nearby.

🤝

Family, Friends and Gifts

People visit relatives, exchange sweets and gifts, call faraway loved ones and share wishes. Diwali is as much about togetherness as it is about light.
